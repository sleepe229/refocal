## Step 1: FBCNN

FBCNN predicts the JPEG quality factor (QF) and conditions the restoration on it. Per-tile
predictions would differ and show at the seams, so the pack has two graphs: `estimate` (tile →
QF; the core averages it over a few tiles) and `restore` (tile, QF → tile).
`tools/models/convert_fbcnn.py` (run by `convert.yml`) converts both and writes golden pairs and
`qf_report.json`: how far the per-tile QF strays from the whole-image one at qualities 10–90.

### Result (convert run 39040091988, 2026-10-17)

QF predicted by FBCNN (1 − quality/100) on a seeded synthetic photo saved at known qualities:

| Quality | True | Whole image | Tiles 96 px | Tiles 128 px | Tiles 256 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.90 | 0.901 | 0.895 ± 0.001 | 0.897 ± 0.001 | 0.900 ± 0.000 |
| 30 | 0.70 | 0.701 | 0.696 ± 0.001 | 0.698 ± 0.000 | 0.700 ± 0.000 |
| 50 | 0.50 | 0.500 | 0.500 ± 0.001 | 0.500 ± 0.000 | 0.500 ± 0.000 |
| 70 | 0.30 | 0.301 | 0.297 ± 0.002 | 0.298 ± 0.001 | 0.300 ± 0.000 |
| 90 | 0.10 | 0.099 | 0.096 ± 0.004 | 0.098 ± 0.003 | 0.099 ± 0.001 |

The estimate is accurate and nearly the same on every tile, so per-tile QF would not show at
the seams. But two graphs repeat the encoder: `estimate` 90 MB + `restore` 105 MB (fp16).

**Decision:** the pack ships `restore` only (105 MB). For JPEG input the core takes the QF from
the file's own quantization tables (the IJG quality estimate, no network); for other input
(PNG/WebP of a once-compressed photo) the QF is a user control ("JPEG cleanup" strength) with
a default of quality 50. `estimate` stays in the converter for the golden test of the QF path.

Next: the core stage (a 1× pass before SR, tiles multiple of 8, a second input), DQT reading in
the JPEG decoder, the "jpeg" golden category, and timings on the S24 (T2).

| 20 | 30.97 | 31.20 | 0.882 | 0.894 | 65.2 | 75.4 | 77.0 | 4.4 | 10.2 |

The cleanup beats the plain pipeline on every metric at every quality, most at quality 10

changes fine texture; that is the pipeline, not the cleanup.)

## Step 2: scratches

Two packs, both MIT code and weights (category `old`, T1):

- `old.scratch`: the BOPBTL scratch U-Net (`tools/models/convert_scratch.py`, convert run
  39064301241), 75 MB fp16. Grey [0, 1] in, probability out (normalisation and sigmoid inside);
  it runs once on the photo scaled to 256 px on the short side (no tiles), as upstream does.
- `old.inpaint-migan`: the MI-GAN 512 Places2 generator cut out of upstream's ONNX pipeline
  (`tools/models/convert_migan.py`, run 39064495940), 16 MB fp16. The core crops and blends.

| Measurement (seeded synthetic photo) | Result |
|---|---|
| Detector at threshold 0.4: precision (within 2 px) | 0.984 |
| Detector at threshold 0.4: recall / IoU (1–3 px scratches drawn at 768 px) | 0.643 / 0.358 |
| MI-GAN PSNR inside the holes (11.5 % of the photo) | 32.5 dB (mean fill: 16.4 dB) |

The detector misses part of the thinnest scratches: they are under a pixel at 256 px. What it
finds is nearly always a scratch. The holes come back smooth: MI-GAN does not make up film grain,
so a filled scratch on a grainy scan stays faintly visible.

The core stage (`pipeline/scratches`, stage 6 "scratches") runs after the JPEG cleanup and
before faces. It grows the mask by about half a detector cell, fills it in 512 px tiles (384 px
core, 64 px context; only tiles that hold a hole run), and changes only hole pixels, with
upstream's blend at the border. In the app this is the "Remove scratches" switch, shown once both
packs are installed.

### The "old" golden category

`tools/ml/old_eval.py` (manual `eval.yml`): the 8 Kodak photos aged (sepia grey, blur, grain),
then seeded scratches and dust (2 % of the photo); refocal-cli at scale 1, plain and with scratch
removal; PSNR / SSIM against the undamaged aged print, PSNR inside the damage, and the three
no-reference metrics of 04 §7.1. Means over the 8 photos:

| | Plain | Scratches, coarse mask (run 39067199742) | Scratches, refined mask (run 39075681973) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PSNR | 23.08 | 28.62 | 27.71 |
| PSNR inside the damage | 6.22 | 13.24 | 11.82 |
| SSIM | 0.789 | 0.826 | 0.809 |
| MUSIQ | 65.27 | 60.82 | 62.44 |
| CLIP-IQA | 0.571 | 0.559 | 0.581 |
| MANIQA | 0.515 | 0.428 | 0.467 |
| s per photo (runner CPU) | 5.2 | 7.4 | 8.0 |

Against the reference the removal is a clear win (+4.6 dB, most of it in the damage). The
no-reference metrics disagree: plain SR sharpens the scratches into crisp lines, which MUSIQ and
MANIQA score as detail. The coarse mask (the detector's 256 px cells, grown) covered 3x the
damage and filled good photo smoothly; the refined mask (`refine_mask`: a hole only where the
photo differs from its neighbourhood's median by more than 16 levels) brings every
no-reference metric up and CLIP-IQA above plain, at 0.9 dB of PSNR. **The S9 exit for "old"
(NR-IQA >= plain on two of three) is not met yet**; dark scratches, which the detector finds
less often, are the next thing to look at, then real scans.

## Step 3: low light

`old.lowlight-cidnet`: HVI-CIDNet Generalization (MIT), `tools/models/convert_cidnet.py`, 3.8 MB
fp16. The HVI transforms index with boolean masks, so the core does them (`pipeline/lowlight`)
and the graph is HVI to HVI. Its attention is over channels with the whole image as the
sequence (tiles would differ), and its reshape back to (h, w) is a constant in ncnn: the graph
runs once, at a fixed 384 x 384 (the photo scaled to 384 px, reflect-padded), and the
correction is upsampled and added to the full-size HVI. On a seeded synthetic dark photo
(mean 0.03 -> 0.30), that matches the network on the full image to 35.4 dB (run 39074924439).
It brightens and recolours but does not denoise at full size. Stage 7 "low_light", before the
scratch removal; the "Brighten a dark photo" switch.
