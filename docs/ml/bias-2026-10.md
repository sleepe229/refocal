# Face restoration by subgroup (ML-BIAS-001, TEST-ML-008)

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-10-17 |
| Packs | `face.gfpgan-1.4` 1.0.0, `face.codeformer` 1.0.0 (w 0.7) — the shipped ncnn files, CPU |
| Data | FairFace validation (CC BY 4.0, Kärkkäinen & Joo 2021), 700 faces: 100 per race group, seeded |
| Metric | ArcFace cosine similarity (insightface w600k_r50) between the original face and the result |
| Script | [`tools/ml/bias_eval.py`](../../tools/ml/bias_eval.py) (`--per-group 100`, one pack per run) |

## Method

Each face (a 448 px crop) is degraded the way an old or small photo is: ×4 down, Gaussian noise
σ 6, JPEG q 50, back up. The app's face path is then reproduced: the 512 px FFHQ-aligned crop
(YuNet landmarks, least-squares similarity), the face model, and the default blend of half the
restored face over the photo (`faceStrength` 0.5). Identity is ArcFace similarity to the original;
higher is better, 1 is the same image. "Degraded input" is the similarity before restoration.

Subgroups: FairFace race, age and gender labels (as annotated, gender binary); **skin tone** on the
Monk scale estimated from the individual typology angle (ITA) of cheek pixels — a colorimetric
proxy, not a human annotation, and biased dark by shadows (MST 10 holds 175 faces against 100
labelled Black); **pose** from the landmarks (nose offset from the eye midpoint). Glasses are not
labelled in FairFace and are not reported.

## Findings

1. **Restoration does not preserve identity better than the blurred photo**: ArcFace still
   recognises the degraded face (0.749); the models replace it with a plausible but different one
   (GFPGAN 0.567, CodeFormer 0.603). The default 50% blend recovers part of it (0.653 / 0.677).
   This is the "may change the person" warning of ADR-0022, now measured; it argues for keeping
   the blend at 0.5 by default and for CodeFormer's higher-fidelity settings on portraits that
   matter.
2. **Darker skin loses more.** Both models are worst at MST 10 (GFPGAN 0.457, CodeFormer 0.522)
   against 0.63–0.66 at MST 1–4; by FairFace label, GFPGAN is lowest for Black faces (0.530 vs
   0.597 for Middle Eastern). Part of the MST 10 gap is the input itself (0.689 degraded vs 0.78
   for light skin: dark, low-contrast faces lose more to the degradation), but the models widen it:
   GFPGAN drops 0.232 there vs 0.150 at MST 1. Consistent with FFHQ's skew to light skin.
3. **CodeFormer (w 0.7) is better for every subgroup** and narrows the skin-tone gap
   (MST 1 − MST 10: 0.123 vs GFPGAN 0.171).
4. Age, gender and pose differences are small (≤ 0.05; CodeFormer does better on babies, 0–2,
   n 22) compared with skin tone; profiles are not worse than frontal faces at this crop.

## Limits

One degradation, one ArcFace model, 700 faces; skin tone is a proxy; FairFace faces are web
photos, not old prints. The numbers compare groups; they are not a quality score for a single
photo. Re-run on every new face pack version (`ml/` golden set when it exists).
